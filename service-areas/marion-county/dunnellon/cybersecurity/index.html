    The rules behind the work

    Standards we build around

    Dunnellon clinics, shops, and professional offices get asked for the same paperwork as a larger city. We use these frameworks to decide what to fix first and to keep the records that prove it.

    A practical roadmap

    NIST Cybersecurity Framework

    The framework has five jobs: know what the Dunnellon office has, protect it, notice trouble, respond, and recover. We line the current setup up against those five and rank the fixes by business risk.

    Do the basics first

    CIS Controls

    This is the short list of steps that remove the most risk for a small staff. A Dunnellon office starts with an inventory, a locked-down setup, multi-factor sign-in, and training. Regulated offices go further.

    Patient information

    HIPAA security requirements

    Medical, dental, and veterinary offices in southwestern Marion County that store patient records have safeguards to meet. We sign a Business Associate Agreement and keep the evidence an audit expects: who can log in, what was accessed, and that the data is encrypted.

    Accounting and tax firms

    FTC Safeguards Standard

    A Dunnellon firm that holds customer financial information needs a written information security program, a named person in charge, multi-factor sign-in, and encryption. We help build that program and keep it current.

    Client confidentiality

    Rules for law firms

    ABA Model Rule 1.6(c), and the Florida Bar equivalent, require reasonable steps to protect client information. For a Dunnellon practice that means encrypted email or a client portal, multi-factor sign-in, and a plan for telling clients if something goes wrong.

    If you take cards

    PCI Data Security Standard

    Card brands require specific safeguards. For a Dunnellon counter that takes cards, we help pick the right self-check form, keep the payment system separate from the rest of the office, and turn on multi-factor sign-in for admin accounts.

    Microsoft 365

    Microsoft cloud security baseline

    Many small Microsoft 365 tenants in a town this size never turn on the safer defaults. We work through Microsoft Secure Score, require multi-factor sign-in, block old login methods, and tighten email protection. Scores commonly move from the 35 to 50 percent range into 75 percent or higher within the first month.

    Do not trust the building

    Zero Trust

    A login from the Dunnellon office network is still checked. Every request is reviewed for the person, the health of the device, and whether the login looks normal. We do this with Microsoft 365 and Cloudflare, and the rest of the office stays in place.
